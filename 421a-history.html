The History of 421-a

Origins

Over the last 50 years, the 421-a tax incentive has played a signficant role in the development of new housing in New York City. As the housing market and political landscape evolved, lawmakers expanded the mission of the program, introducing affordability and wage requriements.

Timeline

  • 1971 The 421-a tax incentive program was created to boost the city's stagnant housing market, giving developers a 10-year property tax exemption.
  • 1984 Added requirement for affordable units in designated areas of Manhattan dubbed the "Geographic Exclusion Area" or GEA.
  • 2007 The GEA expanded to all of Manhattan and parts of Brooklyn.
  • 2016 After a year-long fight between developers and labor unions, the program was reintroduced with new rules that imposed high wage standards in certain geographies, ended the practice of constructing the affordable units off-site, and extended the exemption to 35-years.
  • 2022 With the program again set to expire, the State legislature decided to replace 421-a with 485-x, a similar program with even more stringent affordability and prevailing wage requirements.

Sources: NYU Furman Center.
